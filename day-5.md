# Day 5 – Rocket Competition & Conservation Expo

## Activities

- Competed in the water-rocket launch challenge; my rocket reached **77 feet**.
- Worked with my group to create a final Expo project.
- Developed an animal-conservation plan focused on sea turtles and giant pandas.
- Used AI to support research, organize ideas, and improve the presentation.
- Explained how AI assisted the project and why it should be used responsibly.
- Presented the project during the final gallery walk.

## Rocket Result

The final rocket reached a measured height of **77 feet**. The result showed how the design choices from Day 4 worked together during an actual launch.

## What I Learned

I learned that engineering involves building, testing, examining the result, and deciding how a design could be improved. I also learned more about threats faced by endangered animals and how technology can support conservation work.

While creating the conservation plan, I learned that AI can help with brainstorming and organization, but people must still evaluate the information and communicate it accurately.

## My Favorite Part

My favorite part was presenting the conservation project during the gallery walk. It was rewarding to explain our team's ideas to parents and visitors. I also enjoyed seeing the water rocket reach 77 feet after applying the physics and design ideas from the previous day.

## Final Reflection

The camp showed me that many STEM fields are connected. The week combined AI, biology, environmental science, physics, engineering, teamwork, and communication. My biggest takeaway was that a strong solution requires both technical knowledge and responsible decision-making.
