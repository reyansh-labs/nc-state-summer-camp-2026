# Water Rocket Engineering Challenge

> **NC State Summer Camp 2026 · Engineering Design Project**

## Project at a Glance

| Item | Documented Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Design and build a stable water rocket capable of reaching the greatest possible height during the final launch challenge. |
| **Materials** | Plastic bottle, cork, water, foam-board fins, hand pump, and balancing weight. |
| **Design choices** | Foam-board fins for stability and weight near the top to improve balance. |
| **Final result** | A measured height of **77 feet** during the final launch challenge. |
| **Evidence** | A prototype photograph and launch video are included below. |

## Project Overview

During the camp, I designed, built, and launched a water rocket. The project gave me the opportunity to apply physics, engineering design, and experimental thinking to a working prototype.

## Design

I used foam-board fins to help stabilize the rocket during flight. I also placed weight near the top of the rocket to improve its balance.

The bottle contained water and compressed air. During launch, the compressed air pushed water downward through the bottle opening, producing an upward reaction force on the rocket.

## Engineering Design Process

| Step | What I Did |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Ask** | Consider what makes a water rocket fly high while remaining stable. |
| **2. Imagine** | Think about how fins, weight, balance, water, and air pressure could affect flight. |
| **3. Plan** | Select materials and determine the placement of the fins and weight. |
| **4. Build** | Construct the rocket from the selected materials. |
| **5. Test** | Launch the prototype and observe its flight. |
| **6. Improve** | Identify variables to test in a future design. |

## Physics Connections

| Concept | Connection to the Rocket |
| --- | --- |
| **Thrust** | Water forced downward during launch produced an upward reaction force. |
| **Velocity** | The rocket accelerated upward and then slowed because of gravity and air resistance. |
| **Energy** | The moving rocket had kinetic energy, while gaining altitude increased its gravitational potential energy. |
| **Stability** | The fins, mass distribution, and balance influenced how straight the rocket traveled. |

## Result

The rocket reached a measured height of **77 feet** during the final launch challenge. The method used to obtain that measurement is not documented in the materials currently included in this repository.

## What I Would Test Next

For a second version, I would change one variable at a time and record the resulting launch height.

| Variable | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Water volume** | What amount produces the best flight? |
| **Fin shape** | Does a different fin design improve stability? |
| **Nose weight** | How does mass distribution affect flight? |
| **Overall mass** | How does total mass affect maximum height? |

A controlled testing process would make it easier to determine which design changes improve performance.

## What I Learned

This project taught me that successful engineering is not just about building something once. Testing, measuring, and improving the design are as important as the original idea.

I also learned how concepts from physics can directly affect the performance of a real engineering project.
