# Day 4 – Space Flight & Water-Rocket Engineering

## Activities

- Learned how artificial intelligence can support space exploration.
- Discussed how AI may help spacecraft navigate, plan flight paths, and perform complex maneuvers.
- Studied kinetic energy, velocity, gravitational potential energy, thrust, and stability.
- Designed and built a prototype water rocket.
- Constructed the rocket using a plastic bottle, cork, water, foam-board fins, a hand pump, and weights near the top for stability.

## Engineering Challenge

The goal was to apply physics and engineering ideas to build a stable water rocket that could travel as high as possible. The design required decisions about fin placement, weight, balance, and construction.

## What I Learned

I learned that physics concepts work together during a rocket launch. Thrust begins the motion, while the rocket's mass, stability, and velocity affect its flight. Building the rocket helped me connect classroom concepts to a physical design.

I also learned that AI can assist space missions by helping spacecraft process information, navigate, and make decisions when immediate human control is difficult.

## My Favorite Part

My favorite part was building and testing the prototype water rocket. It was exciting to turn an idea into a design that could be tested.

## Next Step

If I redesigned the rocket, I would test one variable at a time—such as water volume, fin design, or nose weight—and record the height of every launch. This would help me determine which change improved the design.
