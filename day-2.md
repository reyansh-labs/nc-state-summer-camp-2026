# Day 2 – Biotechnology & Water Quality

## Activities

- Visited a biotechnology research center to learn about agricultural research.
- Learned how scientists study corn DNA and use selective breeding and genetic crosses to develop improved crop varieties.
- Explored how biotechnology can improve food production and support agriculture.
- Tested different water samples in a water-quality laboratory activity.
- Learned about turbidity, pH, and other indicators of water quality.
- Used water-testing strips to evaluate different samples.

## What I Learned

I learned how biotechnology is used to develop crops that can be more productive, disease-resistant, and efficient. I also learned that water quality can be evaluated using multiple measurements. Turbidity describes how clear or cloudy water is, while pH indicates how acidic or basic it is.

The water-testing activity helped me understand that scientists use evidence from several measurements before deciding whether an environment is healthy.

## My Favorite Part

My favorite part was visiting the biotechnology research center because I saw how scientists use genetics to improve crops that people depend on. I also enjoyed testing water samples because it demonstrated how environmental scientists collect evidence about water quality.

## My Reflection

Biotechnology and environmental science both use data and experimentation to solve real-world problems. If I repeated the water-quality activity, I would record every result in a table and compare the samples before drawing a conclusion.
